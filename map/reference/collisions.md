## What a row is, and what it is not

A row pairs two names that a person could reach for interchangeably, and says what makes them resemble each other.

**Shared characters are a fact about the names.** Anyone can count them, and a search box returns what it returns.

**Which one a person actually grabs is not a fact about the names.** It is a fact about people, and nothing in the account settles it. Lane B of `catalog.md` carries that open question, and Test B settles it by watching one happen.

No row carries a verdict. A collision is not a complaint about how anything was named (`rules.md` §6).

## Three rows that a character count would miss

Rows 5 and 8 share **no characters at all**, and row 3 shares every word without either object being a copy of the other. Counting shared characters would find none of the three.

- **Row 5** — the two calendars share a screen and a job description. Somebody handed "the appointment calendar" has two true readings of that phrase and no overlap in the names to warn them.
- **Row 8** — `Maria` and `Sarah` collide entirely in speech. The mistake is not made in a search box; it is made in a sentence, before anybody opens anything. And the two are not corrected in the same place: Sarah is changed in this account, Maria in a system outside the boundary `identity.md` draws.
- **Row 3** — identical words on two different kinds of object. A search box returns both, and both are the right answer to "the Fit Call Booked thing."

The pairs that share a long prefix — 1, 2, 6, 7 — are caught by looking carefully at a list. These three are not.
